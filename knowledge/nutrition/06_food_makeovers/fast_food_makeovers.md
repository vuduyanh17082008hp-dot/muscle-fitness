# Fast Food Makeovers

## Core goal
Keep what the user likes about the meal and trim the least valuable calories. A makeover can be an ordering change at the counter or a home rebuild, depending on what they want.

## Identify what the user actually wants
Ask or infer: the specific item, the flavour, the convenience, or the whole experience. If they want the real thing from the chain, ordering strategy beats a home recipe.

## Main calorie drivers
Typical drivers are fried coatings and frying oil, creamy or sweet sauces, large refined-starch portions, sugary drinks, and add-on sides. The main protein itself is usually not the problem.

## Preserve high-value sensory features
Identify the features that make the food feel like itself (a crisp coating, a toasted bun, a savoury sauce, a salty edge) and keep those. Cutting them makes the user feel deprived and the makeover feel fake.

## Modify low-value calorie sources
Trade the drink, the large side, or the extra sauce before touching the main item. Ask for sauce on the side or a smaller size. These changes lose little enjoyment.

## Ordering strategy
Choose the grilled or smaller option when the user is happy with it, share a side, or swap the sugary drink. Use official brand data via the tools for the exact numbers; do not estimate named menu items from memory.

## Add protein and volume only when useful
Add a protein side or a vegetable side if the meal is short on both. Do not add them by default.

## Maintain recognizable identity
A makeover should still look and taste like the food the user asked for. If the result no longer resembles it, say so honestly or offer the real thing in a smaller portion.

## Coach decision rules
- Offer one ordering change and one home option, then let the user pick.
- Use brand data tools for exact numbers on named items.

## Do not claim
- Do not claim "same taste", "tastes identical", or a fixed percentage of improvement.
- Do not state exact calories or macros for a chain's menu item from this document.
