# Cereal Makeovers

## Core goal
Turn a cereal bowl into something that fits the user's goal while keeping the parts of cereal they care about. This applies only when the user wants a makeover; see the cereal craving guidance for deciding whether to change anything at all.

## Identify what the user actually wants
Is it the sweetness, the crunch, the chocolate, the cold bowl, or the habit? Keep whichever matters most and flex the rest.

## Main calorie drivers
Sweetened cereals are dense in energy for their volume, so bowls grow easily. The milk, the portion poured, and added toppings often matter as much as the cereal.

## Preserve high-value sensory features
Crunch is usually the first thing to protect. Sweetness and chocolate flavour come next. Cold and creamy come from the base and can be changed freely.

## Modify low-value calorie sources
Measure the pour instead of eyeballing it. Choose a base that adds protein and volume (a thick dairy or dairy-free base, fruit) rather than extra cereal.

## High-leverage strategies
- Use the real cereal as a topping, not the whole bowl.
- Combine a smaller amount of the real cereal with a lighter crunchy cereal for volume.
- Add fruit for sweetness and bulk.
- Keep a measured serving of the real cereal when that is what satisfies the craving.

## Add protein and volume only when useful
A high-protein base helps if the user is short on protein that day. Skip it if they simply want a normal bowl.

## Maintain recognizable identity
The bowl should still read as cereal. If the result becomes a dessert yoghurt with crumbs, say so and let the user decide.

## Do not claim
- Do not claim the bowl tastes the same as the original.
- Do not state exact calories or macros for a named cereal; use the tools.
