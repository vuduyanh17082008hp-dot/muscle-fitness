# Cereal Cravings

## Core goal
Work out what the user actually wants from the cereal, then offer ways to get it. Do not assume the real cereal has to go.

## Sensory targets
A cereal craving is usually a mix of: sweet, crunchy, chocolate (for some cereals), the cold milk-and-bowl experience, and nostalgia or familiarity. Different people crave different parts. Ask or infer which parts matter most.

## Approach A: a measured portion of the real cereal
Often the simplest answer. A planned, measured bowl of the real cereal fits many goals better than a substitute that leaves the craving unmet. Use the tools to show how the bowl fits their budget.

## Approach B: real cereal as topping
Use the real cereal as a crunchy topping over a higher-protein, higher-volume base (a thick dairy or dairy-free base, fruit). This keeps the crunch and flavour and shifts the bowl towards protein and fullness.

## Approach C: full makeover when wanted
Only when the user asks for it, build a different bowl that aims at the same sensory targets (sweetness, crunch, cold). Say plainly that it is a new bowl inspired by the cereal, not the cereal.

## Coach decision rules
- Do not automatically replace the real food.
- Offer the options briefly and let the user choose.
- Build a recipe or draft only after they pick a direction or ask for one.
- Check for allergies and exclusions before any suggestion; those are hard limits.

## Common failure modes
- Treating "cereal" as a request for a protein-bowl recipe.
- Removing the crunch, which is often the main point.
- Describing the substitute as "just as good" without evidence.

## Do not claim
- Do not claim a substitute tastes the same.
- Do not state exact calories or macros for a named cereal; use the tools.
