# Ice Cream Cravings

## Core goal
Understand which part of ice cream the user wants, and help them enjoy it within their plan. A dessert is allowed to be a dessert.

## Sensory targets
Ice cream is a combination of cold, creamy, sweet, and often a ritual (a treat after a long day, a shared dessert, eating from the tub). The ritual and the creamy-cold texture are often more important than the exact flavour.

## Real ice cream, planned
A deliberately portioned serving of real ice cream is often the most satisfying choice. A small portion eaten slowly in a bowl can feel more satisfying than a larger portion eaten distractedly.

## Higher-protein or lighter alternatives
Blended frozen fruit, thick dairy or dairy-free bases, and frozen high-protein desserts can match the cold-and-creamy target. They are good in their own right and sometimes fit a tight budget better.

## Honesty about substitutes
Do not pretend a protein substitute is identical to real ice cream. Say it aims at the cold, creamy, and sweet targets and will taste and feel somewhat different. Let the user decide whether that works for the craving.

## Coach decision rules
- Ask what they want most (cold, creamy, sweet, ritual) when it is not clear.
- Offer a real measured portion and one alternative; do not push the alternative.
- Respect allergies, lactose intolerance, and exclusions as hard limits.

## Common failure modes
- Immediately producing a fixed recipe.
- Treating dessert as something to be earned or punished.
- Calling a substitute "the same".

## Do not claim
- Do not claim identical taste or texture.
- Do not state exact calories or macros for a named product; use the tools.
