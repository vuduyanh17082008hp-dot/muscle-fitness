# Flavor Hacks

## Core goal
Improve enjoyment of food without relying on calorie-dense ingredients. Flavour is built from a handful of levers; use the cheapest ones first.

## Acidity
Lemon, lime, vinegar, pickles, and fermented elements brighten food and make smaller amounts of fat or salt taste more satisfying. If a dish tastes flat, try acid before adding oil or sugar.

## Salt
Salt (sodium) amplifies other flavours. Season in layers and at the end, and remember that soy sauce, fish sauce, stock and other concentrated sauces are where much of the sodium in a dish sits. Users with a medical reason to limit salt need their own professional guidance; do not override it.

## Sweetness
Fruit, a small measured amount of the real sweetener, or sweet aromatics (cinnamon, vanilla) can carry sweetness. Sweetness expectations are partly habit; a measured portion of the real thing is a valid choice.

## Spice and heat
Chilli, pepper, ginger, and spice blends add intensity and slow eating. They give strong flavour per bite at very little added energy.

## Bitterness
Coffee, cocoa, dark leaves, and char add depth and balance sweetness. Use sparingly so it stays pleasant.

## Umami
Soy sauce, fish sauce, miso, mushrooms, tomato, and aged cheese provide savouriness that reduces the need for fat. Watch the salt in concentrated sauces.

## Aromatics and herbs
Garlic, onion, ginger, lemongrass, and fresh herbs add a lot of perceived flavour and volume for little energy. Toast spices or char aromatics for depth.

## Seasoning and sauces
A strong dry rub or a small amount of an intense sauce beats a large amount of a mild one. Serve sauce on the side so the user controls the amount.

## Coach decision rules
- Diagnose what is missing (acid, salt, sweet, heat, depth) before suggesting an addition.
- Prefer low-energy flavour sources first.
- Respect allergies and exclusions; some sauces contain common allergens.

## Do not claim
- Do not claim a flavour hack makes food taste identical to the original.
- Do not claim physiological effects from any spice or seasoning.
