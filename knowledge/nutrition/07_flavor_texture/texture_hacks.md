# Texture Hacks

## Core goal
Make food more satisfying through texture, since texture is often what a craving is really about, without defaulting to calorie-dense add-ins.

## Crunch
Crunch can come from a small amount of the real crunchy item as a topping, toasted nuts or seeds in a measured amount, raw vegetables, crisp fruit, or roasted chickpeas. Crunch used as a topping keeps its impact at a small portion.

## Creaminess
Creaminess can come from thick dairy or dairy-free bases, blended fruit, blended cottage-style cheese, mashed starchy foods, or a measured amount of real cream or oil. Blending longer usually feels creamier.

## Chew
Chewy textures slow eating and help fullness: whole grains, dense noodles, jerky-style proteins, dried fruit in small amounts.

## Crispness
Crisp coatings come from thin, dry coatings, high heat, and airflow (air fryer or rack-in-oven). A damp coating or crowded tray makes food soggy.

## Volume
Whipping or blending frozen fruit, adding vegetables, or using soups and broths increases volume. Large, airy portions feel more generous at a lower energy load.

## Cold and frozen texture
Freezing changes how sweetness and creaminess feel. Partially frozen fruit, frozen yoghurt-style bases, and chilled bowls hit the cold-and-creamy target for desserts and cereal-style cravings.

## Contrast
Pair a soft base with a crunchy top, or a creamy sauce with a crisp item. Contrast makes a smaller amount of each feel more interesting than one uniform texture.

## Coach decision rules
- Identify the texture the user wants before suggesting a method.
- Check equipment (blender, freezer, air fryer) before recommending a technique.
- Offer the technique, not a fixed recipe.

## Do not claim
- Do not claim that a texture hack reproduces the original food exactly.
